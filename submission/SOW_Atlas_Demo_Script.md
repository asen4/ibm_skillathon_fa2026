# SOW Atlas: 3-minute demo script

About 400 spoken words, which runs 2:40 to 2:50 at a relaxed pace and leaves a few seconds of air for the clicks. Times are targets, not limits. If you run long, the trace step at 1:50 is the easiest thing to cut.

## Before you hit record

Open `examples/project-clearpath/output/Clearpath_SOW_Atlas.html` in Chrome, go full screen (F11 or Ctrl+Cmd+F), and set browser zoom to 90% so the route map fits without scrolling sideways. Reload the page right before recording. It always starts on the Brief, at Week 9, with "Both sides" selected, so a reload is a clean reset. Record at 1920 by 1080 with the mouse cursor visible and move it slowly, because viewers follow the pointer. Do two dry runs out loud. If anything goes wrong on the day, the "Take the tour" button walks the same story on its own.

## The script

### 0:00 to 0:20. The hook

**On screen:** the Brief, scrolled to the top. Don't touch anything yet.

**Say:** "Every IBM engagement starts with a Statement of Work. It decides what we deliver, when we get paid, and what the client owes us. It's also forty pages that almost nobody reads twice. SOW Atlas is a Process Studio skill that reads the SOW once, carefully, and turns it into this."

### 0:20 to 0:45. One screen instead of forty pages

**On screen:** scroll slowly past the headline, the deal strip and the success measures. Click the small § tag next to the first measure, let the drawer open with the clause highlighted, then close it.

**Say:** "This is a fictional SOW I wrote for a health plan modernizing prior authorization. In one screen you get what the project is for, the deal at a glance, and every success measure from today's baseline to target. These little tags open the exact clause each line came from. Nothing is paraphrased on faith. The build checks all 128 quotes against the document, word for word."

### 0:45 to 1:10. What a summary can't do

**On screen:** scroll down to "Settle these before kickoff" and rest the cursor on the first card.

**Say:** "Summaries are the easy part. What matters more is that the atlas reads clauses against each other, and on this SOW it found four problems before kickoff. Here's the first. The client's security team is allowed fifteen business days to review the architecture. But the plan needs that approval two weeks sooner to start the build."

### 1:10 to 1:50. The route map and the slip simulator

**On screen:** click "See it on the map" on that card. When the route map loads, click the "Security review takes its full 15 days" scenario and let the red ripple settle. Point at the impact list on the right.

**Say:** "This is the whole plan as a transit map. Each line is a workstream, each stop is a deliverable, and the tall capsules are decision gates. Now let's give that security review the full fifteen days the contract allows. Everything downstream turns red. All three go-lives move. Three point six five million dollars in payments arrive later. And Final Acceptance now lands three days after the contract ends. Underneath, it tells you what the SOW says happens next, and a few ways to recover."

### 1:50 to 2:05. Trace any stop

**On screen:** click "Put the plan back," then click the large "Pilot live" stop on the blue line.

**Say:** "Pick any stop and it traces the chain. This is the pilot go-live and everything it's waiting on."

### 2:05 to 2:30. The client, and the work itself

**On screen:** press 3 for Client org and pause on the zoom ladder. Then press 4 for Process, scroll to the flow diagram, and click "Play the change."

**Say:** "It also explains the client. From a six point eight billion dollar health system down to the 260-person department this project actually changes, and who speaks for each part. And here's today's process next to the target, with where 410,000 requests a year end up once the new intake and rules are live."

### 2:30 to 2:48. The client's lens

**On screen:** click "LVH view" in the header, press 9 for Who owes what, then drag the "You are here" slider forward a few weeks.

**Say:** "Switch to the client's lens and it becomes their to-do list. Every dated obligation sits on one timeline. Drag the week forward and you can see what's coming due before it's late."

### 2:48 to 3:00. Close

**On screen:** press 1 to return to the Brief.

**Say:** "SOW Atlas works on any SOW, Word, PDF or text, with documented inputs and outputs, and it runs completely offline. One file that both sides can open, trust and actually use. That's SOW Atlas."

## Numbers you'll say, and where they come from

Every figure in the narration appears on screen in the demo, so viewers can see it as you say it. The 128 quotes come from the build output. The four findings are the cards on the Brief. The $3.65M, the three go-lives and "three days after the contract ends" come from the "Security review takes its full 15 days" scenario on the route map (Final Acceptance moves to September 3, and the term ends August 31). The $6.8 billion and the 260 people are on the Client org zoom ladder, and the 410,000 requests are in the Process view's flow diagram.
